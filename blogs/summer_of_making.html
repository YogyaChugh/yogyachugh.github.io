450 hours, 4 ships, one laptop

A rejection, a random email from GitHub, and the best summer I've had.

One random email from GitHub changed my whole summer, and somehow it ended with my dream laptop, completely free.

In June 2025 I was busy surviving semester exams when an email arrived about something called Summer of Making. I had no idea it was about to take over my vacation.

It was run by Hack Club, a nonprofit that helps teenagers learn by building things, and funded by GitHub. The idea was simple: you code, and you earn shells, their in-program currency.

Every hour of coding earned you shells (a VS Code extension tracked the time). When you shipped a project, the community voted on it, and the votes could multiply your shells anywhere from 1x to 30x. You spent shells in a shop full of MacBooks, Raspberry Pis, 3D printers, games, AI tools, headphones and hardware kits. Basically heaven for a builder.

The grind

So I joined, and decided to code my summer away. Over the next three months I built a web scraper (Webber), a game (Timberly), a DOM visualizer that became my first step towards a browser (Webelo), a website and a custom leaderboard.

Locking in that many hours was hard at first, but the thought of something big at the end kept me going. I started with the visualizer, then the scraper (a 150-hour project), then the game (another 70 hours), and kept going.

The event was extended by a month, to the end of September, which gave me time for a few more projects.

The payoff

The timing was funny. I turned 19 on 29 September, right as it ended, so this was my last Summer of Making, and the best one.

I met great people, learned far more than I expected, and logged over 450 hours of coding. By the end I had 6,612 shells and a $1,322 grant, enough for my dream setup: a GIGABYTE AERO X16 with a Ryzen AI 7, an RTX 5070, 32 GB of DDR5 RAM and a 1 TB SSD.

I still can't believe I earned it just by writing code. There was a $25 Steam grant on top, and I finished among the top shell earners of the whole event.

It started with a random email. It ended with me realising how far a few lines of code and some late nights can go.

This was the best summer I've had, not because of what I got, but because of what I learned and built. I'd been let down by Google Summer of Code and a few hackathons that didn't go my way, and this brought the fun of building back. A summer well spent. Thank you, Hack Club.
